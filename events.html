INTO THE WORD

Curated by Andrea Inglese and Gianluca Codeghini.

The event brings together poets, artists, and musicians for a day of readings and actions dedicated to the relationship between word and sound. Among the participants: Carlo Dell’Acqua, Alessandro Broggi, Alessandra Cava, La Centrale Edizioni, Cobra, Ermanno Cristini, Italo Testa, and others.

Marilina Ciaco & Felice Vino present *Assbone*, an audiovisual performance in which the poet’s voice generates images and light in real time. Vino’s video translates rhythm and frequency into visual geometries, turning the reading into an immersive experience that unites poetry and sonic space.

S+T+ARTS / MEET



AQVAM was developed within the European programme S+T+ARTS – Repairing the Present, for the MEET Challenge No. 9 promoted by MEET Digital Culture Center in Milan. The project explores the microbiome of Milan’s aquifers as an ecosystem of co-evolution between nature and technology.

The Cerebro collective — Felice Vino, Antonia Bruno, Giorgio Funaro, and Fabio Volpi — translated biological data into sound, form, and light, creating an immersive environment where water becomes a sensitive organism and a metaphor for the relationship between living matter and digital code.

HUS ART À PORTER


The HUS space once again welcomes a constellation of visual languages — a group exhibition seeking a meeting point between art and the everyday. *Art à Porter* was born to make art accessible, yet within this context my works take a lateral step: they do not seek the object, but transformation.

I brought two bodies. Or perhaps two substances. They are mutating anatomies crossed by organic and digital matter, membranes that breathe and oxidize. In the warm gallery light, they seem to vibrate like unstable organisms — relics of an experiment that continues beyond the frame. The audience stops, observes, discusses. Some search for the technique; others remain silent. In truth, there is no technique. There is a gesture: to hybridize, contaminate, dissolve the boundaries between what lives and what sediments.

Within *Art à Porter*, my research becomes part of a broader dialogue — between craft and artifice, between body and image, between intimacy and exposure. The result is a small visual laboratory where everything is still becoming. And perhaps this is where true accessibility resides: in the instability of form, in its continuous becoming.
